## ElevenLabs block (copy everything below the line)

---

[curious] You find a job that looks perfect. [short pause] An hour later, you realise it asks for nine years of something you have never used. Let me show you a faster way to know.

[matter-of-fact] This is JD Lens, a Chrome side panel that scores any job description against your own master resume, and helps you act on the answer.

Everything starts with your master resume. The Profile tab is who you are. Skills lists what you can claim, with a level and the evidence behind it. And Experience splits each role into small, tagged facts. Those tags are what later powers your Brag Sheet.

[matter-of-fact] Profile management keeps it honest. Open the master resume as a page or a PDF. It is not a normal resume, because it holds far more detail than two pages allow. Rebuild the skill profile after you change your master file, and rescore your saved jobs so their numbers use the latest data.

[excited] Now open a job and click the icon. In a moment you get a keyword score, the salary if it is stated, and flags: remote, location, how old the posting is.

[confident] The ring on the bar is your threshold, the minimum you decided is worth a look. Set it once in Settings, and every job gets a verdict. Worth a look, or below your bar.

[serious] A keyword score is not a verdict, and the tool says so. The Analysis tab adds Claude's judgement: which requirements you meet, which you only partly meet, and which are real gaps. Here a missing must-have pulls the judged score well below the keyword score. That difference is the whole point.

The Details tab holds the job as captured, and a link back to the original posting if you want to apply.

[excited] Then the Brag Sheet. For this one job it picks the work that matters most, shows the proof, prepares an honest answer for each gap, and ends with a one-page revision table you can read just before the interview. It only uses facts from your own master file.

[matter-of-fact] Every saved job lands in Applications. Move it from considering to applied to interviewing, and the history keeps every change.

Switch to another tab and the panel tells you it is still showing the previous job, until you score the new one. Settings holds your threshold, text size, theme, and an optional permission to follow your tabs. It is off until you turn it on.

[warmly] Your data stays in your own folder, and Claude only runs when you ask it to. [short pause] JD Lens. Score the job before you spend the hour.
